Questions

One inverter, one load, or one trolling motor. Inverter amp-hours follow Battle Born's rule of at least 100 amp-hours per 1,000 watts. Load watts are the published Honda and Champion charts. Trolling motors follow Minn Kota's 110 Ah guidance.

  • What size battery bank for a 1000W inverter — 100 Ah at 12 V when one battery can carry the current.
  • What size battery bank for a 2000W inverter — 200 Ah at 12 V, two batteries in parallel.
  • What size battery bank for a 3000W inverter — the 100 Ah per 1,000 W rule applied to a 3,000 W inverter.
  • What size battery bank for a 5000W inverter — the same rule at 5,000 W, above the 3,500 W surge-limiter line.
  • What size battery bank for a CPAP — Champion's 30–120 W range over an 8-hour night.
  • What size battery bank for a refrigerator — Battle Born's 100 W for 5 hours, 500 Wh example.
  • What size battery bank for a sump pump backup — 1,050 W running and 2,150 W starting on a 1/2 hp pump.
  • What size battery bank for an RV air conditioner — 1,800 W running on a 13,500 BTU unit.
  • What size battery bank for a microwave — a 1,500 W draw and 200 Ah with a 2,000 W inverter.
  • What size battery for a 30 lb trolling motor — 30 A at 12 V, at least 110 Ah.
  • What size battery for a 55 lb trolling motor — 50 A at 12 V, one battery, at least 110 Ah.
  • What size battery for an 80 lb trolling motor — 56 A at 24 V, two batteries in series.
  • What size battery for a 112 lb trolling motor — 52 A at 36 V, three batteries in series.

Understanding Topic map

Undersizing a topic map is the #1 off-grid power mistake. The math here uses real usable-DoD cycles, not nameplate ratings.

This tool uses the standard industry formula: it starts from the primary input, applies the safety margin, and rounds up to the next standard size. All intermediate steps are shown on the result card so you can verify the math yourself.

Common mistakes to avoid: reading only the peak rating instead of continuous rating, forgetting headroom for future expansion, or skipping the duty-cycle assumption.
