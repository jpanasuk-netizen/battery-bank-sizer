Deep Cycle vs Lithium Battery for Trolling Motor

Built by Jeremy Panasuk. Published November 17, 2026.

Motor limits and discharge ratings only. No brand pick. The rows are Minn Kota's.

Direct answer: Minn Kota says lithium batteries work with its trolling motors, but brushed motors on lithium should stay at 85% speed (8.5) or lower, because lithium holds higher voltage longer. The battery's continuous discharge rating must cover the motor's maximum draw: Minn Kota lists 30 A for a 30 lb 12 V motor and 50 A for a 55 lb motor.

Continuous discharge checker

Pick the motor and enter the lithium battery's continuous discharge rating from its spec sheet. The checker compares it with the motor's maximum amp draw from Minn Kota's chart. Minn Kota says the battery or batteries must provide enough continuous discharge amperage to run the motor at its max amp draw; when the draw exceeds the battery rating, the battery's management system restricts power and the motor may shut off or act erratically.

The motor list is the max-amp-draw rows from trolling motor amp draw data. Minn Kota says those max values only occur intermittently and should not be used as continuous amp load ratings for runtime; here they are the ceiling the battery's continuous rating has to reach.

Deep cycle or lithium: what Minn Kota says

  • Both work. Minn Kota says lithium batteries can be used with its products, and that QUEST motors are fully compatible with deep-cycle marine batteries as well. Its battery chart recommends a deep-cycle marine battery of at least 110 Ah for best results on a lead-acid setup; the good, better and best rows are on best battery for a Minn Kota trolling motor.
  • Brushed motors and lithium. Lithium sustains higher voltage for longer, which Minn Kota says can be too powerful for motors designed for flooded lead-acid batteries. Running at maximum speed on lithium can cause damage, so it recommends 85% speed (Speed 8.5) or lower.
  • Continuous discharge. A lithium battery's continuous discharge rating has to reach the motor's max amp draw. Minn Kota frames this check around the lithium battery's management system, which restricts power when the draw exceeds the battery's rating.
  • Charging. Minn Kota notes you may not be able to use the same charger for lithium that you used for lead-acid. Check the charger's lithium profile before switching.
