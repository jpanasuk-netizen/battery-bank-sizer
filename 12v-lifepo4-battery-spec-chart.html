12V LiFePO4 Battery Spec Chart

Published October 6, 2026. Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

What this table is: Nameplate figures copied from the manufacturer page or manual linked on that row. Accessed Oct 6, 2026. A blank published field is “not listed.” This page does not estimate a missing watt-hour, amp, weight, cutoff, or cycle count.

Use the Ah column after you already have an amp-hour result from this site’s calculator. The formula is Ah = (Wh/day × autonomy days × 1.25) ÷ voltage ÷ depth of discharge. The weekend cabin inputs are 1,000 Wh/day, 2 days, 12 V, and 0.80: (1,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 260.4167 Ah. Three rows that say 100 Ah are 300 Ah, which covers 260.4167 Ah. That count does not check current. A 2,000 W load at 12 V is 2,000 ÷ 12 = 166.7 A. The BB10012 row lists 100 A max discharge, so one of those batteries does not cover 166.7 A.

Heated and non-heated Battle Born rows share one manual when the manual names both models. Weight and discharge on those pairs are the shared spec table. The cold-charge cell is the line that differs. Renogy’s spec table prints Voltage 12V and the page title prints 12.8V; both are in the voltage cell. Dakota’s technical-spec weight is 27.55 lbs (12.5Kg); a summary line on the same page says 27.5 lbs.

How a missing cell is handled

Battle Born’s 100 Ah, 75 Ah, and 270 Ah manuals do not print watt-hours, so those cells say “not listed.” The Base Series manuals print energy with a tilde, and the tilde stays. SOK’s product page names 100 Ah and 12 V and prints 170 A and 27 lbs; it does not print watt-hours.
