80%

Usable capacity is the only number that matters.

A 100 Ah battery is not 100 Ah you can spend. LiFePO4 gives you about 80%. AGM gives you about 50%. The brochure Ah is a trap.

Bank Wh = Ah × volts. Usable Wh = bank Wh × DoD. Runtime hours = usable Wh ÷ load watts.

Print it. Pick daily watt-hours and days of backup. Divide by depth of discharge. Divide by system voltage. That's the amp-hours to buy — then round up to real battery sizes.

Cabin shorthand

The calculator is Ah = (Wh/day × days × 1.25) ÷ voltage ÷ DoD. For 3 kWh/day, 2 days, and 0.80 DoD, the watt-hours to store are (3,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 0.80 = 9,375 Wh. That is 781.25 Ah at 12 V (9,375 ÷ 12) and 195.3125 Ah at 48 V (9,375 ÷ 48). The directory table prints those as 781 Ah and 195 Ah. Dividing 3,000 × 2 by 0.80 and skipping the 1.25 produces 7,500 Wh, 625 Ah, and 156 Ah, and that shorthand is short of this formula.

Amp-hours and watt-hours

Section added October 3, 2026.

Watt-hours are amp-hours times volts. A 100 Ah battery at 12 V is 1,200 Wh nameplate, which is the first row of the table above. Usable watt-hours are that nameplate times depth of discharge: 1,200 × 0.80 = 960 Wh on LiFePO4, and 1,200 × 0.50 = 600 Wh on AGM. The other direction is the sizing formula. Daily watt-hours go in, amp-hours come out.

Weekend cabin, worked: 1,000 Wh/day, 2 days, 12 V, 0.80. Ah = (1,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 260.4167 Ah, about 260 Ah. Nameplate watt-hours of that result are 260.4167 × 12 = 3,125 Wh, which is also (1,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 0.80. Usable watt-hours at 0.80 are 3,125 × 0.80 = 2,500 Wh, the same 1,000 × 2 × 1.25 the formula started from. At 48 V the amp-hours shrink and the watt-hours do not: 65.104 Ah × 48 V is the same 3,125 Wh.

A common mistake is to stop at “Wh = Ah × V” and buy 2,500 ÷ 12 = 208 Ah, which drops both the 1.25 and the depth of discharge. Another is the 625 Ah figure this section replaces. Shelf loss is not inside either number: the Battle Born spec table’s 2–3% per month, applied once to 260 Ah, is 5.2–7.8 Ah, on how long a battery holds a charge. The AGM case of this same cabin, 416.7 Ah and four 105 Ah batteries, is the AGM guide.
