Quick answer

Formula already on this page: Ah = (Wh/day × autonomy days × 1.25) ÷ voltage ÷ 0.80 DoD for LiFePO4.

Weekend cabin ~1 kWh/day and 2 days of autonomy → about 260 Ah at 12 V (65 Ah at 48 V). Light van ~1.5 kWh/day and 1 day of autonomy → about 195 Ah, two 100 Ah 12 V LiFePO4. Full-timer van at 3 kWh/day and 1 day → about 391 Ah at 12 V.

Frequently asked questions

How many batteries do you need for an off-grid cabin?

A weekend cabin using about 1 kWh/day and 2 days of autonomy needs about 260 Ah at 12V LiFePO4 (65 Ah at 48V) using Ah = (Wh/day × autonomy days × 1.25) ÷ voltage ÷ 0.80 DoD. That is three 100 Ah batteries; a 3 kWh/day cabin at the same 2 days needs about 781 Ah at 12V.

How many batteries for van life?

Two 100 Ah 12V LiFePO4 batteries cover a light 1.5 kWh/day van at 1 day of autonomy (about 195 Ah): fridge, lights, fan, and charging. Full-timer vans at 3 kWh/day and 1 day need about 391 Ah, four 100 Ah batteries.

How many 100 Ah batteries does a 2000 watt inverter need?

A 2,000W inverter can pull 166.7 A from a 12V bank at full load (2,000 ÷ 12). Sustained inverter use means size by daily watt-hours, not inverter rating — 3 kWh/day for 1 day of autonomy needs about 391 Ah at 12V or 195 Ah at 24V.

How long will a 100 Ah battery run a fridge?

This page does not publish a fridge wattage. Use measured daily watt-hours in the formula above. A 100 Ah battery at 12 V is 1,200 Wh nameplate, and the tables plan LiFePO4 at 80% depth of discharge (960 Wh usable).

LiFePO4 vs AGM: how does that change the battery count?

Flooded lead-acid and AGM are typically limited to about 50% depth of discharge, so a 260 Ah LiFePO4 cabin bank (1 kWh/day, 2 days, 12V) becomes about 417 Ah of AGM for the same energy. The Battle Born 100 Ah manual lists 3,000–5,000 deep discharge cycles and 100% usable capacity for that lithium battery; these tables still plan LiFePO4 at 80% DoD. That manual does not publish an AGM cycle count.
