What size battery bank for an off-grid cabin?

The whole answer is one formula plus three honest inputs — your daily watt-hours, your days of autonomy, and your depth of discharge. Everything else is arithmetic.

Quick answer: A weekend cabin using about 1 kWh per day, with 2 days of autonomy, needs about 260 Ah of LiFePO4 at 12V (65 Ah at 48V). A 5 kWh/day cabin at the same 2 days is about 1,302 Ah at 12V. Run the calculator below for your exact load.

The formula (the only one that matters)

Ah = (Wh/day × days of autonomy × 1.25) ÷ system voltage ÷ DoD

Wh/day = everything you'll run, summed · 1.25 = safety margin for inverter losses and aging cells · DoD = 0.80 for LiFePO4, 0.50 for AGM

Run a real load list — don't guess. A cabin fridge cycling 24 hours is not the 100 W on its label; it's label watts × its duty cycle (typically 30–40%). Our battery bank sizer directory keeps the full quick-sizing table updated nightly, but the math below is the whole method.

Worked example: the 1 kWh/day weekend cabin

This is the most common real-world cabin profile — LED lights, a 12V compressor fridge, a water pump, laptop and phone charging, maybe a small TV:

  • LED lights (6 × 10 W × 5 h) = 300 Wh
  • 12V fridge (60 W × 24 h × 0.35 duty) = 500 Wh
  • Water pump (100 W × 0.5 h) = 50 Wh
  • Laptop + phones (150 Wh)
  • Total ≈ 1,000 Wh/day

Now the formula, with 2 days of autonomy and LiFePO4 at 80% DoD:

  • 12V: (1,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 260.4 Ah, about 260 Ah → three 100 Ah 12V LiFePO4 batteries (300 Ah)
  • 24V: same numerator ÷ 24 ÷ 0.80 = 130.2 Ah, about 130 Ah
  • 48V: same numerator ÷ 48 ÷ 0.80 = 65.1 Ah, about 65 Ah

That's the 2-day result. A third day is (1,000 × 3 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 390.6 Ah, about 391 Ah at 12V, or about 98 Ah at 48V.

Three inputs people get wrong

1. The load list

Induction cooktops, well pumps, and space heaters are the cabin killers. A 1,500 W space heater run 4 hours is 6 kWh by itself — six times the weekend-cabin budget. Cabins that heat with electricity stop being battery problems and become generator or wood-stove problems. Audit honestly, and remember duty cycles: anything thermostatic (fridge, freezer, heater) runs a fraction of the clock.

2. Days of autonomy

Every extra day multiplies the bank linearly, and it's the most expensive knob you turn. 2 days is right if you have any charging backup (solar array, generator, a weekly drive with a DC charger). If the bank is the only source during a week of storms, 3 days plus honest load shedding is the real spec. Cabin owners who size 1 day "because we'll just conserve" routinely end up at 100% discharge by night two.

3. Depth of discharge — the AGM trap

AGM is half the price per Ah and roughly half the usable capacity. Flooded lead-acid and AGM are typically limited to about 50% depth of discharge (Battle Born chemistry guide). The Battle Born 100 Ah manual lists that lithium battery at 100% usable depth of discharge and 3,000–5,000 deep discharge cycles; this page still plans LiFePO4 at 80% DoD. That manual does not publish an AGM cycle count. If you're sizing an AGM bank, use 0.50 instead of 0.80 as the DoD divisor. The 1 kWh/day, 2-day, 12V bank is then (1,000 × 2 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.50 = 416.7 Ah, about 417 Ah of AGM, against about 260 Ah of LiFePO4.

Don't forget the surge side

Ah sizing covers energy; the inverter covers power. Add up every motor you might start at once — water pump plus fridge plus tools — and the inverter's surge rating (not its continuous rating) must clear the biggest starter. A pump that runs at 100 W can pull 300–500 W at startup, and a fridge compressor doubles on restart. Sizing the bank without checking surge is the classic cabin mistake: plenty of energy, inverter trips at dawn.

Where solar fits in

Battery banks store; panels refill. If your cabin has an array, the bank and the array are sized against each other: the array should replace a full day's use in your worst month of sun, and the bank covers the nights between. 1 kWh/day with a 400 W array is comfortable in summer and marginal in a cloudy January — which is exactly why autonomy days matters more for solar cabins than generator cabins.

FAQ

What size battery bank do I need for an off-grid cabin?

Using Ah = (Wh/day × autonomy days × 1.25) ÷ voltage ÷ 0.80, a 1 kWh/day cabin is about 130 Ah at 12V for one day (33 Ah at 48V) and about 260 Ah at 12V for two days (65 Ah at 48V). A 5 kWh/day cabin at two days is about 1,302 Ah at 12V or 326 Ah at 48V.

How many days of autonomy should I plan for?

Two days with any charging backup; three if the bank stands alone through storms. Each extra day multiplies the bank linearly — and it's the most expensive knob in the formula, so pair autonomy days with an honest load-shedding plan.

Is 12V or 48V better for a cabin?

Above about 1.5 kWh/day, 24V or 48V wins: a quarter of the Amp-hours, thinner copper, cooler runs, and inverters over 3,000W are effectively 48V-only. Small weekend cabins under 1 kWh/day are the last place 12V still makes sense.

How many batteries for a 1,000 watt-hour-per-day cabin?

The formula gives about 130 Ah at 12V for one day of autonomy and about 260 Ah for two days. Two 100 Ah batteries cover the one-day result (200 Ah). Three 100 Ah batteries cover the two-day result (300 Ah).

Is AGM or LiFePO4 better for an off-grid cabin?

LiFePO4. Flooded lead-acid and AGM are typically limited to about 50% depth of discharge, so a 260 Ah LiFePO4 cabin bank (1 kWh/day, 2 days, 12V) becomes about 417 Ah of AGM for the same energy.
