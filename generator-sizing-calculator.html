What size generator do you need? The 60-second answer, then the details.

Quick answer: add up the RUNNING watts of everything you'll power at once, add the LARGEST starting-watt item (motors take 2–3× their running wattage at startup), and buy a generator rated ~20% above that total. A typical off-grid cabin needs 3,000–4,500 running watts; a home-backup essentials setup needs 5,000–7,500.

Frequently asked questions

What size generator do I need for a house?

For home-backup essentials — fridge, well pump, furnace, lights, router — add the running watts and the largest starting-watt motor, then buy ~20% above that total: typically 5,000–7,500 running watts.

What size generator do I need for an off-grid cabin?

A typical cabin needs 3,000–4,500 running watts. Add the largest starting surge (a well pump can pull 2,100W at startup) and size 20% above the combined total.

What size generator for a 2000 sq ft house?

A portable unit covering essentials runs 7,500–10,000W; a whole-house standby unit for 2,000 sq ft is typically 12,000–18,000W installed (roughly $12,000–18,000).

How many watts does a refrigerator use on a generator?

An energy-star refrigerator runs at about 150W but needs ~600W at compressor startup, so count the starting figure in your generator budget.

Is a battery bank better than a generator?

Under ~5 kWh/day with solar, a LiFePO4 bank beats a generator on noise, fuel, and 10-year cost — the crossover is roughly $4,600 in batteries vs a $1,200 generator plus $80/month of fuel. Hybrid setups beat both for full off-grid.
