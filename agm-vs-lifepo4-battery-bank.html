AGM vs LiFePO4 battery bank

Quick answer: Lifeline says to keep an AGM battery's average depth of discharge at 50% of its 20-hour rating. With this site's 0.50 AGM and 0.80 LiFePO4 factors, 1,000 Wh a day for 2 days at 12 V needs about 417 Ah of AGM or 260 Ah of LiFePO4: four 64 lb Lifeline GPL-31Ts or three 31 lb Battle Born BB10012s.

Run one load both ways

Formula (this site's calculator settings): Wh/day × days × 1.25 ÷ volts ÷ DoD factor. The 1.25 margin and the 0.50 / 0.80 factors are this calculator's settings, not a battery maker's spec. Battery counts use 12 V batteries in series and parallel, rounded up.

What changes between the two

For the same daily load, the AGM bank is larger in amp-hours because you only plan to use about half of it. It is also much heavier. The LiFePO4 bank is smaller and lighter, and Battle Born rates it for thousands of cycles. Charging voltages differ too: the AGM battery guide covers Lifeline's absorption setting, and the 12V LiFePO4 spec chart covers the lithium side.
