How many batteries do you need for van life?

Two 100 Ah LiFePO4 batteries is the answer for most vans. Here's the math that says whether your build is "most vans."

Quick answer: A light weekend van build (1.5 kWh/day, 1 day of autonomy) needs about 195 Ah at 12V LiFePO4 — two 100 Ah batteries. A full-timer build (3 kWh/day, 1 day) needs about 391 Ah, or four 100 Ah batteries. Run the calculator below for your exact load.

The inverter-only version of this question is what size battery bank for a 2000W inverter.

The formula, tuned for vans

Ah = (Wh/day × days × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80

The examples below use 12 V and 0.80. Days = autonomy before recharging.

Those inputs, written as six steps with this page’s 1,500 Wh light-van example, are on how to calculate battery bank size.

Everything hinges on the honest watt-hour audit. Van loads are small individually and relentless collectively: the fridge never turns off, and it's the anchor of every load list.

Two inputs, not an appliance list

This page does not publish a measured van load list. The light example is 1,500 Wh/day and the full-timer example is 3,000 Wh/day. Both are inputs to the formula, the same rows as the table below. A diesel heater's published current is on the diesel heater battery page, not an average invented here.

(1,500 × 1 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 195.3125 Ah, about 195 Ah at 12 V, which two 100 Ah batteries cover. (3,000 × 1 × 1.25) ÷ 12 ÷ 0.80 = 390.625 Ah, about 391 Ah at 12 V, four 100 Ah batteries. The same 3,000 Wh at 48 V is (3,000 × 1 × 1.25) ÷ 48 ÷ 0.80 = 97.656 Ah, about 98 Ah. Series wiring does not multiply amp-hours.

Why the 1.25 and the 0.80 aren't optional

The 1.25 is this site's margin. It is not a manufacturer rating. The 0.80 is the LiFePO4 planning depth of discharge on this site. Skipping either factor changes the amp-hour result relative to every other page here.

Recharging: the other half of the answer

The battery count only matters if you can refill the watt-hours you use. Driving, solar, and shore power are the usual sources. Size each one from the charger or array you actually have. This page does not publish a charge rate or a solar yield.

Frequently asked questions

How many batteries do you need for van life?

A light weekend build (1.5 kWh/day: fridge, lights, fan, phones) needs about 195 Ah at 12V LiFePO4 — two 100 Ah batteries. A full-timer build (3 kWh/day, 1 day of autonomy) needs about 391 Ah at 12V, four 100 Ah batteries.

Is one 100Ah battery enough for van life?

One 100 Ah battery covers the day only when the formula on this page comes out at or under 100 Ah. The light row is 1,500 Wh/day, which is about 195 Ah, so two 100 Ah batteries. Put your own measured watt-hours into the formula rather than a guessed appliance list.

Can you run a 12V fridge off a single battery overnight?

This page does not publish a fridge wattage. A 100 Ah battery at 12 V is 1,200 Wh nameplate. Divide the usable watt-hours (nameplate times 0.80 on the LiFePO4 rows) by the fridge's measured daily watt-hours.

12V or 24V/48V for a van build?

The examples on this page are 12 V. The same watt-hours at a higher voltage are a smaller amp-hour number. Series wiring does not multiply amp-hours.

How long will a 100 Ah battery run a fridge in a van?

A 100 Ah battery at 12 V and 0.80 depth of discharge is 960 Wh usable (100 × 12 × 0.80). Divide 960 by the fridge's measured daily watt-hours. This page does not supply that watt-hour figure.
