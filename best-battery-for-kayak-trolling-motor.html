Best Battery for Trolling Motor on Kayak

12 V motors only: 30 lb, 40 and 45 lb, and 50 and 55 lb. No brand pick. The rows are Minn Kota's.

Direct answer: Minn Kota's smallest motors all run on one 12 V battery and draw at most 30 A (30 lb), 42 A (40 and 45 lb) and 50 A (50 and 55 lb). Its chart recommends a deep-cycle marine battery of at least 110 Ah. A 70 Ah group 24 lasts about 2.3 hours at a 30 lb motor's full draw.

Source for the amps, the 110 Ah line, and the group 24 range: Minn Kota battery and wiring charts, Rev. 8.17.2021. The 2.3 hours is derived on this page as 70 ÷ 30, rounded to one decimal. It is a floor at full draw, not a number printed on the chart.

What the chart calls a good, better, and best battery

Minn Kota's chart says a trolling motor will operate with any lead-acid deep-cycle marine 12-volt battery, and for best results to use one with at least a 110 ampere-hour rating. The same page lists three 12 V run-time rows. None of them is a brand.

Group 24's whole range, 70–85 Ah, sits under the 110 Ah line. Group 27 reaches 110 Ah only at the top of 85–110 Ah. Group 31 is listed at 95–125 Ah, so part of that row is under 110 Ah and part of it is over. The picker is there so a smaller group 24 can be read as a floor at full draw, next to the 110 Ah line.

Where 2.3 hours comes from

70 Ah is the low end of the group 24 row on Minn Kota's chart, and 30 A is that chart's max draw for a 30 lb motor. 70 ÷ 30 = 2.333…, which this page rounds to about 2.3 hours. That division is not printed on the chart. Because the chart says max draw is intermittent, 2.3 hours is the floor at full draw. The same division is what the picker does for any amp-hour number you type.

More amp-hours without leaving 12 V

Minn Kota's chart shows a 12 V system with two batteries wired in parallel "to provide 12-volt system with increased amp hour capacity." The two batteries are to be the same manufacture, type, size, and age. Enter the amp-hours you actually have. The picker still divides by the motor's max draw and still labels the result as a floor.

How to use it

  1. Stay on a 12 V row: 30 lb, 40/45 lb, or 50/55 lb. A 24 V or 36 V motor is a different battery count, covered on the Minn Kota battery page.
  2. Read the breaker off the same row: 50 A at 12 VDC for 30 lb and for 40/45 lb, and 60 A at 12 VDC for 50/55 lb.
  3. Use a deep-cycle marine battery. The chart's best-results line is at least 110 Ah. Group 24 is the good row, and 70 Ah is only its low end.
  4. Treat the picker's hours as a floor. Max draw is intermittent.
